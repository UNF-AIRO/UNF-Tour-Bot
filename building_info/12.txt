Thomas G. Carpenter Library, Building 12

Building 12 is the Thomas G. Carpenter Library, the intellectual heart of the UNF campus. It is a massive four-story structure that houses millions of resources and serves as the primary study hub for students.

Core Details
Official Name: Thomas G. Carpenter Library
Construction: The original building (Phase I) opened in 1980. A major $22.5 million expansion and renovation (Phase II) was completed in 2005, doubling its size.
Primary Function: Academic research, study spaces, and information technology services.

Key Departments & Services
    The library is more than just books; it contains several vital student hubs:
    ITS Help Desk: Located on the first floor, this is the go-to spot for technical support, ID card issues, and software help.
    Special Collections & University Archives: Located on the fourth floor, this area houses rare books, the Eartha M. M. White Collection, and UNF’s historical records.
    Center for Instruction and Research Technology (CIRT): Provides faculty support for digital learning and classroom technology.
    The Writing Center: Offers peer tutoring to help students with essays and research papers.
    Starbucks: A full-service Starbucks is located on the first floor, making it one of the busiest spots on campus.
The "Quiet" Rules
    The library is famously zoned by noise level to accommodate different study styles:
    1st & 2nd Floors: Collaborative study (talking allowed).
    3rd Floor: Quiet study.
    4th Floor: Silent study (strictly enforced).
Historical Significance
    Namesake: It is named after Thomas G. Carpenter, UNF’s founding president (1969–1980), who personally oversaw the university’s creation from a literal "patch of woods" into a functioning campus.
    Architectural Feature: The 2005 expansion added the iconic grand staircase and the glass-walled "reading room" that overlooks the campus lake and woods.
