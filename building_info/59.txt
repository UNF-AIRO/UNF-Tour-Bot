Biological Sciences Building, Building 59

Building 59 is the Biological Sciences Building, a massive 116,000-square-foot facility dedicated to the study of life. Completed in 2012, it is one of the most high-tech academic buildings on campus, designed to support both undergraduate education and cutting-edge faculty research. 

Core Details
    Official Name: Biological Sciences Building
    Primary Occupant: Department of Biology.

Key Features & Services
    Advanced Research Labs: The building features specialized labs for Genetics, Microbiology, and Molecular Biology, equipped with modern tools for DNA sequencing and cellular analysis.
    The Necropsy Lab: A unique, world-class facility used by the UNF Shark Biology Program and marine biologists to study local marine life, including sharks and dolphins.
    Aquatic Center: Includes a sophisticated saltwater and freshwater tank system for research on coral reefs, invasive species, and local fish populations.
    Lecture Halls: Home to three state-of-the-art lecture halls, including a 200-seat auditorium used for large introductory science courses.

Notable Facts
    LEED Gold Certified: Like its neighbors, this building is a sustainability powerhouse, featuring a "green" roof with native Florida plants and a courtyard designed to manage stormwater runoff naturally.
    Coastal Focus: Because of UNF’s proximity to the Atlantic Ocean and the St. Johns River, much of the work in Building 59 focuses on Coastal Biology, making it a premier destination for students interested in environmental conservation.
    The Courtyard: It shares a beautiful outdoor "Science Courtyard" with the Science and Engineering Building (Building 47), creating a massive STEM complex in the campus core.
