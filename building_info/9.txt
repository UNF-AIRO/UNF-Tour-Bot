Frederick H. Schultz Hall. Building 9

Building 9 is officially known as Frederick H. Schultz Hall. It is a major academic hub that originally housed the College of Education and Human Services but now serves a wide variety of departments and student programs.

Core Details
    Official Name: Frederick H. Schultz Hall
    Construction: Built in 1974 and officially opened in 1975.
    Primary Function: General academic building with classrooms, department offices, and dining.

Key Departments & Services
    Since the College of Education moved to its own new facility, Building 9 has become home to several diverse programs: 
    Hicks Honors College: The main offices for the Hicks Honors College are located in Suite 2303.
    Taylor Leadership Institute: Located in Room 1103.
    Department of History: The building houses the primary faculty and administrative offices for the History department.
    Army ROTC: The campus ROTC program is headquartered here.
    Dining Options: Includes a small food court area with options like Ace Sushi and Chick-Fil-A

Historical Significance
    Naming: It was dedicated on May 22, 1992, in honor of Frederick H. Schultz, a Jacksonville civic leader and education advocate who was instrumental in the legislation that created UNF in 1965.
    Frederick Schultz: Beyond his local impact, Schultz served as the Vice Chairman of the Federal Reserve from 1979 to 1982.
    Original Purpose: For over 30 years, it was the primary home for the College of Education before the college moved to Petway Hall (Building 57).
